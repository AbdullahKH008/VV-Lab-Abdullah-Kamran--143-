# Defect Taxonomy

| Category | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ambiguity | A statement has more than one reasonable interpretation. | S5 does not define who qualifies as a “requesting party.” |
| Vagueness | A term lacks a measurable boundary. | S6 uses “safe temperature” without a numeric limit. |
| Incompleteness | Necessary conditions, cases, or data are missing. | S1 does not state how depositor registration is verified. |
| Contradiction | Two requirements cannot both be satisfied under some stated condition. | S7 requires at most -18°C while S8 permits warming to 0°C during maintenance, without defining the exception precisely. |
| Non-verifiability | Compliance cannot be objectively tested from the wording. | S12 says “good audit trail” without specifying required fields or retention. |
| Redundancy | Requirements repeat or substantially overlap without adding distinct meaning. | S6 and S7 both address temperature, but S6 has no independent measurable criterion. |
| Infeasibility | A requirement may be impossible to satisfy as stated or under its constraints. | S7’s “never” may be infeasible during equipment failure unless failures and allowed exceptions are specified. |
| Untraceability | A requirement has no clear link to a stakeholder need, design element, or verification method. | S2 says “large number” without a measurable target that can be traced to a capacity test. |
